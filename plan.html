RINGLESS is a bet that a better coaster is worth paying for. Here is exactly what we're betting, how we'll check it, and what would make us stop.

Testing has not started · results will be posted here

The hypothesis

People with nicer furniture who use coasters at home will pay about $28 for a set of 4 weighted, absorbent, minimalist stone coasters. They'll pay because their current coasters drip and feel cheap.

If any part of that is false, the business fails. The parts are the target customer, the pain, the price, and whether the product works.

Target customerThe painThe priceIt works

The minimum version

Just enough to test the hypothesis, and nothing more.

PART 1

One hand-built prototype set

Diatomite stone top, a stainless steel plate and a cork base, at about 7 to 8 oz each.

PART 2

One landing page

The product pitch, the $28 target price and an email signup button. You're looking at it.

Skipped on purpose: extra colors, packaging and logo polish. None of them test the hypothesis.

Four tests

TEST 1 · ABOUT 1 WEEK

Does the product work?

  • Cold drink on a wood surface for 30 minutes.
  • Soak the prototype for a week to check for rust.

Pass: no ring, no drip-through, no rust bleed.

TEST 2 · 20+ PEOPLE

Interviews

  • Only people with nicer furniture.
  • They hold the prototype next to a cheap coaster.
  • We ask if they'd pay $28, then ask for their email.

The email counts. The verbal yes doesn't.

TEST 3

Landing page signups

  • Shared with interviewees and a wider group, such as classmates' families.
  • Friends and family are tagged and counted separately.

Measure: share of visitors who sign up.

TEST 4

Price split

  • Half of visitors see a lower price, half a higher one.
  • We keep the price with the higher signup rate.

One change at a time.

What would make us stop

These thresholds are our own picks, not industry standards. We're writing them down before testing so we can't move them afterward.

ResultSignal
Prototype passes the wet and rust testsContinue
30% or more of interviewees give their emailContinue
Under 10% give their emailKill or pivot
Rust or ring failure in the product testDrop the plate, try a thicker stone

We'll publish the numbers here once we have them, including the ones we don't like.
